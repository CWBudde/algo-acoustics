# Validation

Call `scene.Validate` before propagation. It aggregates scene errors for:

- a positive sample rate and a non-empty, ordered band specification below
  Nyquist;
- a valid positive-volume shoebox or mesh, defined material references, and
  finite absorption, scattering, and transmission coefficients with
  `absorption + transmission <= 1` in every band;
- either the legacy `room` field or at least two `rooms`, but never both;
- valid portal room indices, material references, planar boundary polygons,
  and winding from the first room index toward the second;
- finite source and receiver positions inside exactly one room; and
- supported receiver types, with a non-nil, sample-rate-matched HRTF for every
  binaural receiver.

Scene validity does not imply that every engine supports every cardinality.
The shipped ISM engine supports one or more sources and exactly one receiver;
the ray-trace engine and `RenderProgressive` require exactly one source and one
receiver. Ray counts, render duration, bounce limits, receiver radius, and
other engine configuration are checked by their owning pipeline. Progressive
rendering validates the complete scene/configuration before its first update
and uses the scene's sample rate and band specification for all tiers.
Cross-room transmission currently requires one source and one receiver in
different, directly connected shoebox rooms.

The `roombench report` corpus in `testdata/rooms` is a deterministic internal
regression corpus. Its T60/EDT/C80 envelopes in `cmd/roombench/report.go` are
codebase baselines with intentionally broad drift tolerances, not published
measurements or third-party ground truth. The external mesh WAV golden has its
separate provenance and refresh procedure in
[external-tool-compatibility.md](external-tool-compatibility.md).

## Diffraction Validation

There is a checked-in 60-point finite-wedge corpus generated by the

official Svensson EDB2 implementation at 50 Hz, 500 Hz, 5 kHz, and 10 kHz. The
fixture, generator, runtime provenance, and hashes are documented in
[`testdata/diffraction`](../testdata/diffraction/README.md); `geometry` checks
every magnitude against a 0.5 dB limit. Additional deterministic tests cover
the BTME total-field view/shadow transition, integrated DAPDF detector-energy
continuity, and the 1--3 dB second-order gain in the canonical L-room fixture.

## Modal Validation (IBM/FDTD)

`pde/ibm_validation_test.go` scores the immersed-boundary FDTD solver against
closed-form eigenfrequencies for three fixtures. The scores are identical on
amd64 and arm64:

| fixture          | band      | recall | chance | threshold |
| ---------------- | --------- | ------ | ------ | --------- |
| shoebox          | 30–153 Hz | 67 %   | 39 %   | ≥ 60 %    |
| triangular prism | 30–187 Hz | 35 %   | 27 %   | ≥ 30 %    |
| cylinder         | 20–147 Hz | 36 %   | 28 %   | ≥ 30 %    |

**Read the margin over chance, not the threshold.** `logChanceLevel` reports how
much of the analysis band the tolerance windows cover — 81 % for the shoebox and
97.5 % for the prism. At that density every peak the solver produces matches
something, so counting matched peaks measures the mode set's density rather than
the solver. Scoring is therefore `requireModeRecall`: how many analytical modes
the solver actually produced. That gets harder, not easier, as the mode set
thickens, and the test fails outright if recall ever falls back to the chance
level. The shoebox's 67 % against 39 % is the strong claim; the prism and
cylinder clear chance by about 8 points, which is real but weak.

**Why the bands are narrow.** Scored over the full 30–500 Hz range, the prism and
cylinder fall _below_ their chance levels — the peaks actively avoid the
analytical frequencies there, so no threshold over that band could mean anything.
Lengthening the run does not rescue it: at 2 s the resolution improves to 0.5 Hz
and the prism's set resolves into 73 clusters, but the solver still yields only
40 peaks, capping recall at 55 % by construction. Both are scored on their sparse
low bands instead, where the windows cover ~27 % of the band.

### Rules for anyone changing these tests

- **Consume each peak at most once**, and cluster analytical modes closer
  together than the resolution before scoring. Letting every mode scan the whole
  peak list independently lets one peak satisfy dozens of modes — with ~40 peaks
  against the prism's 953 modes, that inflates recall several-fold.
- **The equilateral triangle's eigenfrequencies are Lamé's**
  `2c/(3L)·√(m²+mn+n²)`, not `c/(3L)`. The latter puts every transverse mode of
  the prism at half its true frequency, comparing the solver against frequencies
  the room cannot produce.
- **`besselPrimeZeros` must be computed to the band edge**, not read from a fixed
  table. A 13-entry table stopping at angular order m = 4 omits whole mode
  families, including roots inside its own range.
- **Match tolerance is `max(0.5 %·f, 1.5·df)` with `df = 1/(nSteps·dt)`** — the
  true resolution, not the zero-padded FFT bin spacing.
- **`nSteps` derives from the timestep the solver actually uses.** `runFDTD`
  takes a duration in seconds and computes the step count from
  `0.95·stencil.CFLLimit(c)`.

### The DC offset

A sealed rigid room has nowhere to put the source's injected volume, so the
receiver's time series settles on a DC offset whose bin runs ~49× the strongest
acoustic mode. A peak threshold taken from the _global_ maximum then rejects
nearly every mode — the shoebox drops from 40 detected peaks to 5.
`extractPeakFreqs` subtracts the mean, applies a Hann taper, and takes its
threshold from the in-band maximum. This only became visible once the walls were
correctly rigid: the earlier pressure-release boundary directions
(see `pde/ibm_grid.go`) vented the enclosure and masked it.
